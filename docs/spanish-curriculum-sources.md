# Free Spanish curricula and worksheets for Krabsy: sources, licences, recommendation

**Short answer:** Yes. The best fit by far is **Oak National Academy's Spanish curriculum** (England, free, government-funded). It covers ages 7 to 14 in a full sequence, with slides, worksheet PDFs, starter and exit quizzes, and a free API with a JSON bulk export. Content published after 1 Sep 2022 is under the **Open Government Licence v3.0**, which allows commercial use and adaptation with attribution and has no share-alike. Its Year 3 sequence opens with *estar*, then *ser*, then *tener*, which is almost exactly Krabsy's current Spanish topics. Most US college open textbooks are NonCommercial and can't be used. A few are CC BY: Semillas and the USG Elementary Spanish eBook.

Licence status key: **[V]** = I read it on the source itself. **[S]** = taken from search results or a secondary listing, not confirmed on the source. **[U]** = could not verify.

### C. Free but not open (use for inspiration only)

ProfeDeELE, SpanishPlayground, Liveworksheets, Twinkl (including the free tier), Lingolex, Kerry Spanish and BBC Bitesize Spanish are **free to use in class, but not licensed for reuse or redistribution**. Twinkl's and the BBC's terms restrict use to personal or non-commercial purposes, and Liveworksheets content belongs to the teachers who uploaded it. I found no CC licence for ProfeDeELE [U]. We can learn from their **topic order and exercise types** (matching, gap-fill, picture labelling, information-gap, find-someone-who). We may not copy their sentences, images, layouts or worksheets.

## 2. Recommendation: build on three or four sources

### Source 1: Oak National Academy (spine, sequence and exercises)
- **Adopt Oak's KS2 sequence (Y3 to Y6) as the children's track, and Oak KS3 Y7 to Y8 for ages 11–14 and adults.** It is open, grammar-driven and developed in the UK, and it already orders estar, ser, tener, hay, -AR verbs, gustar, ir. Its design also targets the "estoy five times" problem. NCELP-style tasks make the learner use the grammar to get the meaning: who is speaking, singular or plural, which ending. A run of identical items is designed out.
- **Get the Spanish JSON bulk export** through the free API key ([bulk download](https://open-api.thenational.academy/bulk-download)). It holds the starter and exit quiz questions with answers and the misconceptions for every lesson. That is a ready, reviewed item bank to convert into Krabsy "gap topic" files. Adapt the wording and add `hint`/`teach` lines.
- **Rules:** use only Collection 2 content. Skip anything marked third-party, especially images in slides. Don't use the Oak name or logo to promote Krabsy. Don't sell access to Oak lessons as they are. Our adapted, interactive versions inside Pro are allowed under OGL. **Before release, confirm that the Spanish lessons are Collection 2** (the curriculum was rebuilt in 2023–24, so they very likely are) [U].
- **One gap:** Oak is UK-oriented (Spain-focused culture, British English in the instructions). It also seems to have no separate audio files, since the asset list names no audio [U]. Krabsy would supply its own Latin-American-safe wording and audio.

### Source 2: Semillas (CC BY, if the licence holds) and USG Elementary Spanish (CC BY 4.0, confirmed) for the adult track
- Use them for adult themes (vivienda, gustos, trabajo) and H5P exercise patterns. **Resolve the Semillas BY vs. BY-NC-SA conflict first** by opening its copyright page in a normal browser. If it is NC-SA, use it for inspiration only.

### Source 3: StoryWeaver (CC BY 4.0) for picture books and readers
- Filter for CC BY Spanish Level 1–2 books. Adapt them into Krabsy picture books and check each book's licence line. African Storybook and Bloom are second choices, CC BY titles only.

### Source 4: Tatoeba CC0 export plus Fluent Emoji (MIT) for extra sentences and vocabulary pictures
- Tatoeba gives Spanish–English example sentences for the gap items. Use the CC0 subset so no per-sentence credit is needed. Fluent Emoji gives animals, food and feelings pictures with no share-alike.
- **Avoid ARASAAC** (NonCommercial). Also avoid OpenMoji and Mulberry for core content because of share-alike (see Risks).

### Attribution text we would need
On a site-wide "Credits / Licences" page, plus a short line in each worksheet's footer:

- Oak (Oak's required statement, with "adapted" added because we change the lessons): *"Adapted from a Spanish lesson by [Oak National Academy](https://www.thenational.academy/), licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Changes: rewritten as interactive exercises."*
- USG eBook: *"Includes material adapted from* Elementary Spanish I & II Interactive eBook *by Dennis Miller, University System of Georgia, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changes made."*
- StoryWeaver: *"[Title] by [author], illustrated by [illustrator], translated by [translator], Pratham Books / StoryWeaver, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Adapted by Krabsy."*
- Tatoeba (if we use non-CC0 sentences): *"Example sentences from Tatoeba (tatoeba.org), CC BY 2.0 FR"*, keeping the sentence IDs in our data files.
- Fluent Emoji: keep the MIT copyright notice ("© Microsoft Corporation") on the credits page.

## 3. Risks

- **Share-alike (BY-SA: OpenMoji, Mulberry, Wiktionary, Trayectos).** Any worksheet that *adapts* SA material must itself be published under CC BY-SA. Anyone, competitors included, could then copy that worksheet, even commercially, with credit, and we can't stop them. It does not cover the site code or unrelated worksheets, but where the line falls (an unmodified SA image placed in our worksheet) is a legal grey area. Keep SA material out of Pro content.
- **NonCommercial (ARASAAC, Acceso, SGIC, Libro Libre, NCELP, some StoryWeaver, African Storybook and Bloom titles).** None of it can be used once tutors pay. Free-tier use alone is risky too, because the same site sells Pro. Treat it all as inspiration only.
- **Mixed licences inside one source.** Oak's third-party images, StoryWeaver's per-book licences, and Semillas' conflicting statements. For each source we adopt, record the licence of each item in our `content/` data (a `source` + `licence` field per question or story).
- **Ideas are free, text is not.** We can always follow a *sequence* or an *exercise type* (CEFR, Plan Curricular, ProfeDeELE). Copying *wording* needs a licence.

## 5. What still needs a human

- **A native-speaker review of every adapted item, ideally by someone from the River Plate region** (Uruguay/Argentina). Oak is Spain-oriented (vosotros, "ordenador"), and Tatoeba and StoryWeaver translations vary in quality. This also covers the Krabsy rule on regional words (e.g. no "concha").
- **A pedagogical check of each worksheet for "give-aways":** no answer revealed by a neighbouring item, a mix of persons and forms, at most two identical answers in a row. Oak and NCELP items are a good model for this.
- **Licence checks a human must do in a browser:**
  1. Confirm the Oak Spanish lessons are Collection 2, and read the API terms when getting the key.
  2. Read Semillas' copyright page (BY vs. NC-SA).
  3. Check the licence line of each StoryWeaver, African Storybook or Bloom book used.
  4. Twemoji and Noto licences, if used.
  5. Trayectos' licence (the site failed for me).
- For a paid tier, a short legal sanity check of the credits page is cheap insurance.
