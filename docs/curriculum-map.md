# English grammar by school year: Germany and Spain

**What this is for:** teachers ask "what fits my Klasse 6 / my 1º ESO?". This
map says in which year each English grammar topic is usually taught in
Germany (Sekundarstufe I, Klasse 5–10) and Spain (5º–6º Primaria, 1º–4º ESO),
and proposes a Krabsy topic plan in teaching order.

**Short answer:**

- **Germany:** the Länder curricula are fairly concrete and agree on the
  core order: be / have got / present simple / present progressive in
  **Kl. 5**; simple past, comparison, going to / will, present perfect,
  if type 1 in **Kl. 5–6** (Gymnasium) or **Kl. 6–7** (Realschule/Gesamtschule);
  if type 2, passive, relative clauses, past perfect in **Kl. 7–8**; reported
  speech, gerund/infinitive, if type 3 in **Kl. 8–9**. Gymnasium is about
  one year ahead of the other school types from Kl. 6 on.
- **Spain:** the national LOMLOE decrees name **no grammar structures at
  all** (only "communicative functions" like "narrar acontecimientos
  pasados"). The order comes from the regions (Madrid lists structures per
  course) and above all from the textbooks. Spanish 1º ESO books cover
  roughly what German Kl. 5–6 does (Gymnasium) in one year, and the
  present perfect lands at the end of 1º or 2º ESO in the books but only in
  3º ESO in the Madrid decree.
- **Rough equivalence for Krabsy's audience (10–14):** DE Kl. 5 ≈ ES 5º–6º
  Primaria + start of 1º ESO; DE Kl. 6 ≈ ES 1º ESO; DE Kl. 7 ≈ ES 2º ESO;
  DE Kl. 8 ≈ ES 3º ESO; DE Kl. 9 ≈ ES 4º ESO. Only use it as a rough guide.

## How to read the main table

- **DE Gym** = Gymnasium (all checked states are G9 now, see caveats).
  **DE other** = Realschule / Gesamtschule / Sekundarschule. A value like
  "6 (Gym 5)" in the text means "Kl. 6, Gymnasium already Kl. 5".
- NRW and the KMK only give **stages** ("by the end of Kl. 6", "by the end
  of Kl. 8", "by the end of Kl. 10"), not single years. Bayern and Hessen
  give single years.
- **ES** = curso where the structure is first taught. "Prim" = Primaria.
- **Agreement:** *usually* = sources agree within one year; *varies* =
  sources differ by two or more years or by school type.
- Source keys are listed under [Sources](#4-sources).

**Grundschule (Germany, Kl. 3–4) as baseline [M]:** English starts in
Kl. 3 in most Länder (Kl. 1 in a few). Grammar is learnt in chunks
("I like …", "Can I have …?") and is not taught explicitly; Kl. 5 starts
again from zero, with to be. I did not read a Grundschule curriculum
for this map. The BW sample curricula for Kl. 5/6 come in two
versions, with and without primary-school English [S], which shows the
baseline differs.

**Spanish Primaria (5º–6º) baseline [V, Madrid only]:** Madrid's Primaria
decree already lists present simple, present continuous, simple past and
going to for the **2º ciclo (3º–4º)**, and adds **will** in the **3er
ciclo (5º–6º)**. It also lists comparatives and superlatives, have got,
the possessive 's, can, have (got) to, should and much/many. So Spanish
children start ESO having met many of the structures that German
children first meet in Kl. 5–6. In practice 1º ESO revises them all.

## 3. Proposed topic plan (English, teaching order)

Rules applied (CLAUDE.md, `content/FORMAT.md`): no grammar terms in the
question; only English in the question, no hint in brackets; the sentence
alone must settle the answer, also when typed. The standard trick for
tense topics is to **put the same verb in the sentence in another form**
(`I walk to school every day. Yesterday I ___ to school too.` → walked),
or to give a time word that allows only one answer. Wrong choices = the
other forms of the same verb.

**Format:**

- **gap** = gap topic, format v2, works today in every `quiz` game.
- **chain** = positional form chain like `go → ___ → ___`. Today the
  engine's `chain` shape exists only for `irregular_verbs`. New chain
  topics need engine work, or can be written as gap topics with an
  arrow prompt as a first step (`big → ___` is a valid one-gap sentence).

"Year" = typical year of first teaching: DE Gym / DE other / ES. Teacher
names can use grammar terms (teachers know them); the question text cannot.

## 5. Caveats

- **Germany, G8 / G9.** Bayern (from the late 2010s [M]), NRW (KLP in
  force from 2019 [V]) and now BW (from 2025/26, Kl. 5–6 first [S]) teach
  the nine-year Gymnasium. The BW
  Bildungsplan 2016 was revised on 10.03.2025 for G9, and the years in the
  BW row are from the 2016 version via a school document. Older G8
  textbooks still in use move topics one year earlier.
- **Germany, states.** Real differences: simple past is Kl. 5 in Bayern
  but Kl. 6 in NRW / Hessen / BW. Bayern Gym puts defining relative clauses
  and question tags in Kl. 6 and reported speech in Kl. 9, while Hessen has
  reported speech without backshift in Kl. 7. NRW only fixes stage ends
  (6, 8, 10), so inside a stage the textbook decides.
- **Germany, school types.** Realschule / Gesamtschule are about one year
  behind the Gymnasium from Kl. 6 or 7 on (e.g. if type 2: Gym 7, LH 8;
  if type 3: Gym 7–8, LH 9). Gesamtschulen split into Grund- / Erweiterungskurs
  from Kl. 7–9, and some topics (question tags) appear only in the
  Erweiterungskurs. Hauptschule / Mittelschule was not checked.
- **Germany, textbooks decide the real order** inside the stage.
  Teachers think in "Unit 3 of Access 2" more than in curriculum years.
  A Krabsy page per textbook unit would be the most precise, but would need
  the textbook tables of contents (not public as text).
- **Spain, competence-based.** RD 157/2022 and RD 217/2022 name functions
  and "unidades lingüísticas", not structures, and group courses in pairs
  (1º–2º, 3º–4º ESO; ciclos in Primaria). Only regions that add their own
  concretion (Madrid does, per course) give years. Elsewhere the
  publisher's book is the de facto curriculum.
- **Spain, Madrid decree vs textbooks.** The decree is more conservative
  than the books: present perfect in 3º ESO (books: end of 1º or 2º ESO),
  for/since in 4º (books: 1º–2º), relative clauses / passive / reported
  speech only in 4º. Teachers follow the book, so Krabsy should present
  "usually 1º–2º ESO" and not quote the decree as the rule.
- **Spain, Primaria.** Madrid already lists simple past and going to in
  3º–4º Primaria. Children arrive in 1º ESO having met most basics, and
  1º ESO books revise everything from to be on, in one year.
- **Bilingual schools** (Madrid "programa bilingüe", many Spanish schools)
  and German bilingual streams are ahead of all of this.
- **Equivalence DE ↔ ES** in the summary is my inference from the tables
  above [M]. It is good enough to sort Krabsy topics but not to tell a
  teacher what their class "should" know.
